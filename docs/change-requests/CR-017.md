# CR-017: Fail-over and the crew LAN (M11)

- **Status:** approved 2026-10-10 by engineering under the product owner's standing delegation.
  No new feature: the design, timing and dependencies of F-SYS-04, F-SYS-05 and R-OPS-02. A CR
  is required because `caelum-cluster` is network and crypto code (CLAUDE.md, CR-003 §5).
- **Affects:** `caelum-cluster`; later the core (event replication hooks) and the shell (instance
  role in the status footer, kiosk station).

## 1. Model
There is one primary and one standby. The primary appends every station event to a signed,
hash-chained, sequenced event log and replicates it to the standby. Fencing tokens (epoch, node)
are totally ordered: a node that has seen a higher fence refuses a lower one, and a primary that
sees a higher fence demotes. An event counts as **acknowledged** only after the standby
persisted it. No acknowledged entry is lost across a takeover. Unacknowledged entries of a
fenced, partitioned primary are quarantined, never merged.

## 2. Transport and dependencies
- Replication and the crew LAN run over **mutual TLS 1.3** (rustls with the `ring` provider,
  TLS 1.3 only). The crew-LAN CA is the only trust anchor, and a client certificate is required
  on every connection.
- Replication messages are JSON text frames over a WebSocket. Integrity does not depend on the
  encoding: each entry is verified by its Ed25519 signature and chain.
- Crates: `axum` 0.8 (HTTP/1 and WebSocket server), `hyper` 1 and `hyper-util`, `tokio-rustls`
  0.26, `tokio-tungstenite` 0.29, `rustls` 0.23 and `ed25519-dalek` 3. All are permissive and
  pure Rust; `ring` builds C and assembly with the existing toolchain. `rcgen` is a dev
  dependency, for test certificates.

## 3. Timing (R-OPS-02)
Defaults:
- heartbeat every 1 s;
- the standby promotes after 5 missed heartbeats;
- the driver ticks every 100 ms;
- devices get a 4 s budget to reconnect to the new primary.

That is a worst case of 9.1 s, within the 10 s limit. A timing configuration whose worst case
exceeds 10 s is refused (test `r_ops_02_timing_beyond_10_s_is_refused`).

## 4. Station identity
Every station is provisioned in a `StationRegistry`, pinned by the SHA-256 fingerprint of its
certificate, with a kind: peer, secondary or kiosk. An unprovisioned certificate is dropped
before any HTTP is read.

## 5. LAN endpoints and sessions (F-SYS-05, CLAUDE.md rule 6)
| Endpoint | Who |
|---|---|
| `GET /v1/status` | every provisioned station |
| `POST /v1/sessions`, `DELETE /v1/sessions` | secondary stations (sign-in of a user there) |
| `GET /v1/alarms`, `/v1/alarms/stream` | a signed-in user on a secondary station, with the same rule as the `alarm_list` IPC command; the view is projected per role and crew scope (MCC: OPS fields only) |
| `GET /v1/emergency/stream` | secondary and kiosk stations (crew-wide emergency view, no values) |
| `GET /v1/cluster/replicate` | the peer only |

User session tokens:
- 256-bit, random, bound to the issuing station's certificate; only their SHA-256 is kept;
- refused from another station, after 10 min idle (the onboard auto-lock), after 12 h, after
  sign-out, and when the account is no longer active.

A standby serves status but no crew data.
